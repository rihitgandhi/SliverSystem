Our Mission

Your accessibility settings should follow you.

Second Brain stores supported preferences like font size, contrast, motion reduction, and spacing, then applies them as you browse on websites that allow extension changes.

The moment

The setup should not restart on every site.

Picture someone who needs larger text and less motion. On one site they find the setting and change it. On the next, there is no setting. On a third, there is one, but it forgets them tomorrow.

The fixes are well understood. What’s missing is an owner.

Every site assumes accessibility is someone else’s job: the next sprint, the next redesign, the next person who touches the code. I think nobody owns it, so nothing changes.

One number

95.9%

of the top one million home pages had detectable accessibility failures in 2026.

WebAIM Million, automated analysis, February 2026.

The same report found that about 96% of detected errors fell into six categories: low contrast text, missing alt text, missing form input labels, empty links, empty buttons, and missing document language.

What I’m building

I’m not waiting for a million websites to change.

I’m building the fix where it can reach the person browsing: the browser. Second Brain does not repair broken page code. It gives supported accessibility preferences a place to live and a way to travel across the sites where an extension can work.

Second Brain

Set supported preferences once. Carry them forward.

Second Brain applies supported preferences as you browse. Some browser pages and restricted surfaces do not allow extensions to run, so those pages may not support every feature.
