Introducing

Second Brain

It remembers how you like to read the web, so you never set it up twice.

Text size, contrast, and line spacing — set once, applied automatically on every site after that.

The problem

Accessibility settings that don't survive a closed tab aren't accessible.

Larger text. Higher contrast. Wider line spacing. Get it right on one site, and the next site you open forgets everything — you're back to square one, every single time.

For most people that's a minor annoyance. For someone who depends on these settings to read at all, it's friction repeated dozens of times a day.

How it works

Four small steps, and you never think about it again.

01

You adjust a setting

Bump the text size, switch on high contrast, widen the line spacing — on any site, using the controls already there.

02

Second Brain saves it, locally

The choice is written to your browser's own storage on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere.

03

You open a different site

A new domain, a new page, no relationship to the last one — the kind of jump that normally resets everything.

04

It's already set

Your text size, contrast, and spacing are applied before the page finishes loading. No popup, no setup, no reminder needed.

Trust and control

What Second Brain sees — and what it never does.

People who rely on accessibility tools are often more wary of tracking, not less. So here's the full list, not a footnote.

What it stores

  • Your preferred text size
  • Whether high contrast is on
  • Your preferred line spacing

What it never touches

  • Browsing history or page content
  • Names, emails, or any personal identifiers
  • Anything sent to a server — there isn't one

Everything above lives only in your browser's local storage, on your device. No account, no sync, no server round-trip.
