Second Brain

You set up your accessibility needs on one site. Then you do it again on the next.

Second Brain is built for the gap between what people need and what most websites remember.

The problem

The web is still asking people to work around the same failures.

WebAIM's 2026 audit of the top 1 million homepages found 95.9% have detectable WCAG 2 failures, averaging 56.1 errors per page. Users with disabilities encounter an error roughly once every 26 page elements.

The same six error types have caused 96% of all detected accessibility issues for seven consecutive years. The problem is not getting fixed site-by-site, no matter how long the web waits.

Site-by-site

Every website decides whether to remember, expose, or respect the settings a person needs.

The turn

Instead of waiting for every website to fix itself, put the fix in the browser.

Second Brain carries accessibility preferences with the user. The settings live where they can follow someone from page to page, automatically.

How it works

Set your preferences once. Second Brain applies them as you browse.

1

Choose your settings.

Font size, contrast, motion reduction, spacing, and related accessibility preferences start in one place.

2

Visit a website.

Second Brain detects the page you are on and checks what can be adjusted.

3

Keep moving.

The extension applies supported preferences automatically, reducing repeated setup from site to site.

Voice Navigation

Hands-free browsing belongs in the same accessibility system.

Voice Navigation is a feature within Second Brain. It lets a user control browsing hands-free, reducing reliance on a mouse, keyboard, or touch input.

It matters because navigation is part of accessibility, not an add-on after the reading settings are finished.

Within Second Brain
  • Move through pages with spoken control.
  • Act on browsing tasks without pointer input.
  • Keep accessibility preferences and navigation support together.
