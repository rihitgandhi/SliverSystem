Research

AI can help accessibility, but only with limits.

Research lens Scale Standards Evidence Limits

01

AccessAI starts from evidence: disability is common, barriers repeat at scale, and technical compliance still requires human judgment.

AI can reduce friction by helping apply a user's own preferences more consistently. It cannot replace accessible design, standards-based engineering, testing with assistive technology, or decisions made with disabled people.

95.9%
WebAIM 2026

95.9% of the top one million home pages had detectable WCAG 2 failures.

The automated analysis found 56.1 accessibility errors per page on average, up 10.1% from 2025. Because automated tools detect only a subset of barriers, a page with no flagged errors is not necessarily accessible or conformant.

Recurring failures

Six error types account for about 96% of detected issues.

Low contrast, missing alternative text, missing form labels, empty links, empty buttons, and missing document language have remained the dominant automated findings for seven years.

1/26
Page complexity

One detectable error appeared for every 26 home-page elements.

WebAIM analyzed more than 1.4 billion elements. The average home page contained 1,437 elements in 2026, nearly twice the 2019 average, showing how growing interface complexity can multiply opportunities for barriers.

Who it affects

More than 70 million U.S. adults report having a disability.

CDC data from the 2022 Behavioral Risk Factor Surveillance System puts the figure above one in four adults. Accessibility is therefore core infrastructure, not a niche accommodation.

70 million+
Global scale

About 1.3 billion people experience significant disability.

The World Health Organization estimates that disability affects 16% of the global population—roughly one person in six—and emphasizes that environmental barriers help shape disability outcomes.

Standards baseline

WCAG 2.2 adds nine success criteria, but does not cover every user need.

The additions address issues such as focus visibility, target size, dragging alternatives, consistent help, redundant entry, and accessible authentication. W3C still cautions that conformance alone cannot meet every need.

Public-sector requirements

U.S. state and local government sites must meet WCAG 2.1 Level AA.

The Department of Justice's Title II rule sets compliance dates of April 2027 for larger public entities and April 2028 for smaller entities and special districts. Technical compliance does not remove the broader duty to provide effective communication and equal access.

Image-description research

Useful image descriptions depend on context, not a single universal template.

Interviews with 28 people who were blind or had low vision found that the information people wanted changed with the task and setting, including news, shopping, employment, social media, and productivity tools.

28 participants
AI limits

Fluent descriptions can still be wrong.

NIST identifies confabulation—confidently presented false or erroneous content—as a core generative-AI risk. For accessibility, that means generated descriptions and recommendations should expose uncertainty, preserve user control, and remain subject to human review.
